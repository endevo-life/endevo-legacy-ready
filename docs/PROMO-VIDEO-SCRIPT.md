# My Final Playbook — 90-second promo script

**For:** the video slot on `/solutions`
**Runtime:** 1:30 (hard ceiling 2:00)
**Format:** 3D animated motion graphics, no live action, no voice actor on camera
**Aspect:** 16:9 master, 1:1 and 9:16 cutdowns from the same scenes

---

## The one idea

Everyone in this category sells _peace of mind_, which is unfalsifiable and
therefore invisible. This sells something concrete and rarer: **knowing the
order.** The visual argument is scattered → sequenced. Chaos resolving into a
line. Every scene either builds the mess or resolves it — nothing decorative.

**Tone:** calm, warm, quietly confident. Not sombre, not chirpy. This is about
life admin, not death. If a frame feels like a funeral, cut it.

**Do not say:** "AI-powered", "estate planning", "everything you need",
"complete". All four are either untrue, unwinnable, or invite a comparison we
lose.

---

## Palette & look

|          |                                                                                    |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ground   | Deep navy `#0A1F3D` — the brand navy, used as negative space                       |
| Accent   | Warm orange `#FF6B00` — the resolved path, and _only_ the path                     |
| Neutral  | Warm off-white `#F4F1EA` for cards and type                                        |
| Material | Soft-shadowed matte cards floating in shallow depth. No glass, no chrome, no neon. |
| Camera   | Slow, deliberate dollies. One hero move per scene. Never handheld.                 |
| Type     | Playfair Display for the two title cards, Open Sans for labels — matches the site. |

## Script

### Scene 1 · 0:00–0:10 — The pile

**Visual:** Black. A single document card fades up, floating. Then another.
Then twelve more, faster, drifting out of alignment — a will, insurance, a
password list, a deed, a photo album, a bank statement. They crowd the frame
until they overlap into noise. Camera pulls back slowly; the pile keeps growing
past the edges.

**VO:** "You know you should get your affairs in order."

**On screen:** _(nothing — let the pile do the work)_

---

### Scene 2 · 0:10–0:20 — The freeze

**Visual:** Everything stops mid-drift. Total silence for a beat — one full
second of held frame. The cards hang there, unsorted.

**VO:** "Almost nobody knows where to start."

**Sound:** Music drops out entirely on "start." The silence is the point.

---

### Scene 3 · 0:20–0:32 — The question

**Visual:** The pile dims to 20% opacity and pushes back in depth. A single
clean card slides forward into focus, centred, with one plain question on it.
The card is warm off-white against the navy — the first thing that has felt
calm.

**VO:** "So we start somewhere else. We ask about your life."

**On screen:** A question appears, types out, and is answered. Then a second
card replaces it. Then a third — each one visibly _different_ because of the
last answer.

---

### Scene 4 · 0:32–0:48 — The sort

**Visual:** The hero move. The dimmed pile behind snaps into motion — each
document flies to one of five labelled columns that rise from the bottom of
frame. What was chaos becomes five clean stacks. Orange path-lines draw between
them in sequence, connecting first → second → third.

**VO:** "Then we build a plan around it. Not a checklist built for everyone —
one built for you."

**On screen (column labels, appearing as each fills):**
`LEGAL` · `FINANCIAL` · `DIGITAL` · `HEALTH` · `YOUR PEOPLE`

---

### Scene 5 · 0:48–1:02 — The pace

**Visual:** Camera moves along the orange path. Milestones sit on it as small
markers. Three fill with a soft orange glow, one after another — then the
camera keeps moving and the rest are still ahead, unfilled. A calendar chip
appears beside the path, showing a date. A hand-cursor drags the date later;
the path stretches to accommodate it and nothing breaks.

**VO:** "Work through it at your own pace. Set your own deadline — and move it
when life does."

---

### Scene 6 · 1:02–1:14 — The return

**Visual:** Time-lapse feel. The path recedes into the distance, complete. Then
a new marker appears _on it_ — the path extends. Small icons pulse beside it: a
house, a stethoscope, a baby.

**VO:** "And come back when something changes. A move. A diagnosis. A new
grandchild. Your plan changes when your life does."

**On screen:** `A plan, not a document.`

---

### Scene 7 · 1:14–1:24 — The Playbook

**Visual:** The whole path collapses gracefully inward and binds into a single
closed book — matte, warm off-white, the orange path visible as the spine.
It rotates once, slowly, and settles. Everything else falls away to navy.

**VO:** "At the end, you have your Playbook. Yours to keep. And your family
knows exactly what to do."

**On screen:** `MY FINAL PLAYBOOK`

---

### Scene 8 · 1:24–1:30 — The card

**Visual:** Book scales down to a mark. Logo and CTA resolve beneath it. Hold
on a clean, still frame — no motion on the final beat.

**VO:** "Start free. Get your invite at endevo dot life."

**On screen:**

```
Know exactly what you need to do,
and in what order.

endevo.life/solutions
Start free · $100 when you're ready
```

## Production notes

**Sound.** One piano-and-strings bed, warm, no percussion until Scene 4 where a
soft pulse enters with the sort and carries to the end. The Scene 2 silence is
the single most important audio decision in the piece — protect it in the mix.

**Voice.** One narrator, unhurried, mid-warm. Roughly 135 words at this length,
which is a relaxed read — resist the urge to add copy to fill space. The pauses
are doing work.

**The 30-second cut.** Scenes 1, 2, 4, 7, 8. Drops the pace and revision beats,
keeps chaos → order → Playbook → CTA.

**The 9:16 cut.** Recompose, don't crop. The five columns in Scene 4 become a
vertical stack; the Scene 5 path runs bottom-to-top instead of left-to-right.

**Accessibility.** Burn in open captions on the social cuts. Every on-screen
line must clear 4.5:1 against the navy — the orange at `#FF6B00` passes on
`#0A1F3D`, but check any tint of it before shipping.

**What to avoid.** No stock-3D clichés: no rotating gears, no glowing brains,
no network-node constellations, no ticking clocks. No gravestones, no candles,
no wilting flowers — this is not a bereavement product. If a frame would look
at home in a life-insurance ad from 2010, rebuild it.
